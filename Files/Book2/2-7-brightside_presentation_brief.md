# Presentation Brief — Brightside Learning Q2 Scheduling Recommendation

**Meeting:** 7-minute presentation + Q&A
**Audience:** Managing Director, Program Manager
**Decision requested:** Approve scheduling priorities for next quarter — which courses/formats to increase, maintain, or reconsider

---

## 1. What the audience needs to understand

- The analysis covers 28 workshop sessions run April–June 2026, across four courses (AI at Work, Excel Essentials, Present with Confidence, Project Planning) and two formats (Online, In-person), evenly split at 7 sessions per course and 14 sessions per format.
- Three metrics were used to judge performance: net revenue per session, attendance rate (bookings that actually showed up), and satisfaction (1–5 scale).
- No single course or format wins on all three metrics — the recommendation requires a trade-off, which is exactly what the MD/PM need to weigh in on.
- The raw data had quality problems (duplicate row, inconsistent text, a missing refund figure, a bookings-over-capacity anomaly) that were cleaned or flagged. Two issues remain unresolved and put a ceiling on how confident we can be in the exact numbers, even though the direction of the findings is reasonably clear.

## 2. The decision I am asking them to make

Approve a next-quarter scheduling stance along these lines:

- **Increase:** Project Planning and AI at Work (strongest revenue per session; AI at Work also leads on attendance and satisfaction).
- **Maintain:** Present with Confidence (solid satisfaction and mid-tier revenue; no strong signal to change volume).
- **Reconsider / hold:** Excel Essentials (lowest revenue per session, lowest attendance rate, lowest satisfaction of the four).
- **Format tilt:** Favor in-person where feasible for attendance reliability (93% vs. 87% show-up rate), while recognizing online still pulls more total bookings and slightly higher average revenue per session — so this is a "lean," not a wholesale shift away from online.

I'll ask them to sign off on this direction, not final session counts — those would follow in a scheduling exercise afterward.

## 3. The evidence that will matter most (keep these front and center)

- **Net revenue per session, known-refund sessions only:** Project Planning ~$1,968 (highest) → AI at Work ~$1,827 → Present with Confidence ~$1,539 → Excel Essentials ~$1,382 (lowest).
- **Attendance rate by course:** AI at Work ~94% (highest) → Present with Confidence ~90% → Project Planning ~89% → Excel Essentials ~86% (lowest).
- **Satisfaction by course:** all in a narrow 4.2–4.7 band — real but not a strong differentiator on its own.
- **Format comparison:** In-person converts bookings to attendees more reliably (93% vs. 87%) and edges out online slightly on satisfaction (4.58 vs. 4.35); online generated more total bookings (297 vs. 233) and a marginally higher revenue-per-session average ($1,719 vs. $1,613).
- **Excel Essentials is the consistent laggard** — lowest on all three metrics — which is the clearest, lowest-risk part of the recommendation.

## 4. Questions or objections the audience may raise — and how to handle them

- **"Is this based on a full quarter or a fluke month?"** → Data spans all three months (Apr–Jun) evenly, but it's still only one quarter and one sample per course/format combination in most cases — flag this as a limitation, not a rebuttal-proof case.
- **"What about cost — is this profit or just revenue?"** → The workbook only tracks fees, bookings, and refunds; there is no instructor cost, venue cost, or overhead data, so this is revenue per session, not profitability. Be upfront that cost isn't in scope.
- **"Why exclude Excel Essentials if it still has 86% attendance and 4.2 satisfaction — that's not bad?"** → Acknowledge it's not failing, just the weakest of four solid options; "reconsider" means hold steady or trim slightly, not cancel.
- **"Can we trust the Project Planning revenue number given the missing refund?"** → Yes, be direct about this: session S-107 (Project Planning, Austin) is missing its refund amount and was excluded from all net revenue math, so Project Planning's average is based on 6 of 7 sessions. If that missing refund were large, the course's true average could be lower — worth flagging as a caveat on the strongest headline number.
- **"What's going on with the session that had more bookings than seats?"** → Session S-119 (Project Planning, Denver) shows 23 bookings against a listed capacity of 20 — unexplained, not corrected. Recommend confirming with the data owner before finalizing counts; it currently doesn't materially change the direction of the recommendation but is worth naming as an open item.
- **"Why lean in-person if online has more bookings and similar revenue?"** → Frame it as attendance reliability (fewer no-shows, better room utilization and attendee experience) rather than a revenue argument — the revenue gap between formats is narrow.

## 5. Information that can be left out (given the 7-minute limit)

- Individual session-level detail (session IDs, dates, individual notes) — keep these in the appendix/backup only, in case a question calls for it.
- The full data-cleaning log (capitalization fixes, duplicate row, trailing spaces) — mention that cleaning happened and flag only the two unresolved issues that affect the numbers (missing refund, over-capacity booking); skip the cosmetic fixes entirely.
- Location-level breakdowns (Austin/Chicago/Denver/Remote) — not part of the business question and not summarized in the Analysis sheet; don't introduce a new axis of analysis that wasn't part of the original scope.
- Gross revenue figures — net revenue is the more decision-relevant number; gross can stay in backup.
- Attendance vs. capacity utilization — capacity isn't part of the three requested metrics; only raise it if the S-119 anomaly comes up in Q&A.

## 6. Tone and level of detail

- **Confident but calibrated.** This is a fictional-but-realistic internal audience that knows the business better than the data — lead with the recommendation, not a data walkthrough. Don't hedge on the clear findings (Excel Essentials trailing, in-person's attendance edge), but be explicit about the two open data-quality items rather than glossing over them — credibility with this audience depends on showing you know the limits of the numbers.
- **Rounded numbers only in the room** (~$1,970, ~94%), with exact figures available if asked.
- **Recommendation-first structure:** state the ask in the first minute, use the middle for the three-to-four evidence points above, and reserve the last minute for the two data caveats and the explicit "what would change this" (confirming the missing refund and the capacity anomaly).
- Avoid dwelling on methodology (how attendance rate or net revenue were calculated) unless asked — this audience doesn't need the formulas, they need the implication.

## Data limitations to disclose (for reference)

1. **Missing refund — session S-107** (Project Planning, Austin, April): net revenue not calculable, session excluded from all revenue averages. Understates the sample size behind Project Planning's leading revenue figure.
2. **Bookings exceed capacity — session S-119** (Project Planning, Denver, June): 23 bookings vs. 20 seats, unexplained and uncorrected. Could indicate a capacity data error or an overbooking practice worth understanding before scheduling decisions are finalized.
3. **Missing satisfaction score — session S-111** (Project Planning, Remote, May): feedback form wasn't sent; excluded from satisfaction averages only (minor impact).
4. Small sample: 7 sessions per course and 14 per format, one quarter only — treat findings as directional, as the workbook itself notes.
5. No cost data: recommendation is based on revenue and attendance, not profitability or instructor/venue cost.
